      #1

      What If You Could See Who Has a Crush on You?

      largo-18-01 · que_pasaria_si

      ⚠️ Silencio de 1.3s en el segundo 415 (entre «you.» y «It's»)

      Would you want to see your own number? 👇

      What if you could see who has a crush on you? A pink number floats above every head and thin threads show who likes whom. Follow the consequences through high school, work, friendship and the whole world, until a stranger on the subway changes everything.
      Fun fact: studies suggest most people underestimate how much others like them, a gap psychologists call the liking gap.
      #whatif #crush #psychology #storytime #thecuriolab

      Por qué esta idea

      It's a what-if built around a universal desire: knowing who likes you. It's told in second person, a format the owner already approved ('What If You Fell…', 'What If You Dug…'). The tension escalates through four levels (school → work → friends → society), each with its own small twist that's easy to relate to. That gives the story a clear reason to exist and avoids the 'aimless' problem of the rejected video. The final twist flips the premise (you stop being the observer and become the observed), invites rewatching and comments like 'I'd want to know who it is', and the last line loops back to the hook.

      Guion
      1. What if you could see who likes you?
      2. You wake up, and a glowing pink number floats above every head. It counts the people secretly crushing on them.
      3. Thin pink threads link every admirer to their crush. Only you can see them. Stay until the end, because there's a twist.
      4. You stumble to the bathroom mirror and look up. Your own reflection has no number. Remember that, because it comes back later.
      5. On the street, a jogger runs past with a pink two above her. Two strangers are quietly hoping she notices them.
      6. A man in a suit has a seven. Seven people think about him constantly, and he's yelling at a parking meter.
      7. An old lady feeding pigeons has a one. A thin thread drifts across the park to a man on a bench.
      8. She doesn't know. He doesn't know. And you're the only person on Earth who knows, which feels like a heavy secret.
      9. Your heart pounds. You can't stop staring. Hundreds of threads cross the city like pink spiderwebs, tying everyone together.
      10. So what happens when one ordinary person knows everyone's secret feelings? Let's follow it through school, work, friendship, and the whole world.
      11. Your high school hallway lights up like a pink fireworks show. Numbers everywhere, and threads crisscrossing the lockers.
      12. The most popular guy in your class, the quarterback everybody worships, has a big fat zero. Nobody likes him that way.
      13. His smile looks confident, but you realize something sad. Being popular and being liked are two completely different things.
      14. Then you spot the quiet kid who eats lunch alone every day. Above his head glows a seven.
      15. Seven people secretly adore him. He has no idea, and he thinks nobody would ever notice him.
      16. A thread from a cheerleader, one from a band girl, and one from the guy who runs the school paper.
      17. You almost walk over and tell him. But you freeze. How do you explain something this impossible to a stranger?
      18. Then you see your crush walking toward class. Your heart starts racing as you search for the thread leading to you.
      19. You follow the pink line from your crush's head. It stretches across the room, over desks, past the windows.
      20. It doesn't point at you. It points straight to your lab partner, the one who borrowed your pencil yesterday.
      21. Your stomach drops. Your crush likes your lab partner, and you're the only person in the room who knows.
      22. Do you tell them? Do you sabotage it? Or do you just sit there, holding the biggest secret in school?
      23. You decide to stay quiet. But all day, the threads keep tugging at you, and every glance feels like a tiny movie.
      24. By lunch, you've learned more about your classmates than a hundred diaries could ever tell you.
      25. And you start noticing the quiet kid. Seven threads. Every one of them stuck in silence, waiting for someone to be brave.
      26. So you do something small. You tell the band girl, "Hey, he likes music too." Just a nudge. That's all.
      27. By Friday, they're sitting together. Out of nowhere, your little nudge sparked something. That felt amazing. But it was only the beginning.
      28. Monday morning, you walk into the office, and suddenly the coffee machine feels like the least interesting thing there.
      29. Your boss, a stern manager who never smiles, has a three above her head. Three quiet admirers, and she has no clue.
      30. Then you notice her own thread, going out. It stretches across the office and lands on the new intern, twenty-two years old.
      31. Your boss is crushing on a twenty-two-year-old who reports to her. That's a lawsuit waiting to happen.
      32. Over the next week you watch her hover near his desk. She's giving him the best projects, and the whole team notices.
      33. You can't ignore it any longer. You walk into the HR office, close the door, and say, "We need to talk."
      34. HR investigates quietly. Within a month, your boss is moved to another department, and the intern is safe.
      35. Your company is stunned, because you stopped a problem before anyone filed a complaint. Nobody even knew there was one.
      36. Next thing you know, you're called into the director's office. A shiny new title, a raise, and a corner desk.
      37. You're promoted. Confetti, cupcakes, and a standing ovation in the break room. But then someone whispers, "How did you always know?"
      38. Suddenly every coworker looks at you differently. Some admire you. Some are scared. And a few are asking too many questions.
      39. Someone in the cafeteria asks, "Okay, seriously, do you read minds?" You laugh, but your hands sweat under the table.
      40. And you realize something unsettling. A power that helps people can also make them afraid of you.
      41. Your best friend since third grade calls, screaming with joy. Her boyfriend is about to propose, and she wants you at dinner.
      42. You hug her in the living room, and she's glowing. Above her head is a four, and she's never looked happier.
      43. Then her boyfriend walks in with flowers. His number is a two. But the thread he sends out doesn't point at her.
      44. It stretches out the window, down the street, and loops all the way to a coworker named Jessica.
      45. Your stomach twists. He's about to promise forever to your best friend while his heart is pointing somewhere else.
      46. You could stay silent and let the wedding happen. Or you could break the happiest moment of her life.
      47. That night, you can't sleep. You stare at the ceiling, counting every memory you've shared with her.
      48. The next day, you ask her to meet at the park. Your voice shakes. "I have to tell you something awful."
      49. You explain everything: the threads, the numbers, and the one that leads to Jessica. She just stares at you.
      50. "That's insane," she whispers. "You expect me to believe this?" Her eyes fill with tears, and she steps away.
      51. She confronts her boyfriend that same night. He denies it, then goes quiet, and finally admits he's been texting Jessica.
      52. The engagement is canceled. But instead of thanking you, your best friend blocks your number.
      53. Mia says you ruined her life. She says she'd rather have been happy and wrong than hurt and right.
      54. You sit alone on the couch, tears running down your face. You told the truth, and it cost you your best friend.
      55. That's the cruel part of this power. Truth doesn't always feel like a gift. Sometimes it feels like a weapon.
      56. So you stop warning people and watch in silence. But the power is too tempting, and you start thinking bigger.
      57. One night you have an idea. If you can't say it out loud, maybe you can build something that does.
      58. You build a matchmaking app. You call it Pink, and the home screen asks, "Who likes you? One hundred percent accurate."
      59. The app guesses who likes you, and it's never wrong, because you secretly feed it everything you see.
      60. The first hundred users are the testers. They log in, see the names, and send shaky texts. Every single guess is right.
      61. Within a week, ten thousand people. Within a month, ten million. Your phone buzzes nonstop with news alerts.
      62. Dating apps start to collapse. Swiping suddenly feels pointless when a machine tells you exactly who's secretly into you.
      63. Investors line up outside your door. Your company's worth over a billion dollars, and you're not even thirty yet.
      64. One headline reads, "Dating Apps Collapse: Meet the Love Oracle." Suddenly, your face is on every screen in America.
      65. Strangers thank you in coffee shops. Couples credit you at their weddings. Someone even names a puppy after you.
      66. Plenty of people hate you too. They say you killed romance, that love should be a mystery, not a spreadsheet.
      67. But at night, alone in your penthouse, one detail keeps bugging you. You look in the mirror, and above your head: nothing.
      68. You've told millions of people who loves them. You've never once found out who loves you.
      69. You search the empty air above your reflection. Maybe zero? Maybe a hundred? There's no way to know.
      70. And the more famous you get, the more you wonder if anyone is crushing on you for the real you.
      71. It's past midnight. You leave a gala downtown, skip the limo, and walk toward the subway alone.
      72. The street is empty. Your footsteps echo. Somewhere behind you, another set of footsteps matches your rhythm.
      73. You push through the turnstile and step into an almost empty subway car, flickering with tired yellow light.
      74. At the far end, a man sits in a hood, eyes locked on you. He isn't looking at your face.
      75. He's staring at the empty space above your head. Slowly he stands up, and the train screeches around a curve.
      76. He sits across from you and whispers, "You see them too, don't you?" Your blood goes cold.
      77. You can't answer. For the first time, you realize you aren't the only one with this power.
      78. He smiles gently. "I've watched you for months. I saw every interview. Every launch. I knew you'd figure it out."
      79. "Your number is something you can never see," he says. "But I can. And it isn't zero."
      80. He leans closer. "Yours says one. And the thread goes to me."
      81. The lights flicker. A thin pink line glows in the air, running from his chest straight to you.
      82. Now you know two things: who likes everyone, and that someone's been watching who likes you.
      83. Would you want to see your own number? Tell me in the comments, and watch our next What If video.
