      #1

      Last Person on Earth: 5 Movie Myths That Would Get You Killed

      largo-29-01 · pedido

      ✅ Revisión automática: todo correcto

      Which myth surprised you most? Ferrari or bicycle? 👇

      What would really happen if you were the last person on Earth? We break down 5 Hollywood myths about the end of the world, from the power grid to the subway floods to the twist no movie shows you.
      The New York subway pumps remove about 13 million gallons of water a day, and without them the tunnels could flood in roughly 36 hours.
      #whatif #lastpersonalive #movievsreality #thecurilab #facts

      Por qué esta idea

      It copies the formula of two approved videos ('Movies vs Reality' and '5 Myths vs Reality'). It also avoids the timeline that made the owner reject the 1,000-year version. Each myth feeds a fantasy the viewer has had (free mansions, free cars) and then destroys it with a surprising, verifiable fact, which keeps people watching to the next myth. The ISS twist challenges the title itself and invites comments ('I never thought of that'). It also creates a natural loop: the last line, 'look up', ties back to the opening question.

      Guion
      1. What if you were the last person alive?
      2. Hollywood loves this fantasy: empty cities, free cars, nobody giving orders. But Hollywood got almost everything wrong, and some of it could get you killed.
      3. Today I'll break five myths about being the last person alive. Every one of them feels true, and every one falls apart fast.
      4. Stick around until the end, because the final twist changes the whole question. No movie ever shows you this part.
      5. So wake up. No alarm rings. Your phone says no signal, no messages, nothing. You step outside, and the whole street is silent.
      6. No cars. No voices. Not even a barking dog. Just you, and an entire planet with nobody left to share it.
      7. Let's start with the fantasy every movie sells you first. The one about electricity. Myth number one.
      8. Myth one: you'd have free electricity forever. Every light on, air conditioning blasting, every screen in the city glowing just for you.
      9. In the movies, the hero flips one switch and a whole skyscraper lights up like a Christmas tree. Easy, right?
      10. Here's the reality. Power plants need people. Operators and engineers adjust the output and balance the grid every single minute of the day.
      11. Without them, supply and demand drift apart. Safety systems trip, lines disconnect, and whole sections of the grid shut down automatically.
      12. Within hours to a few days, most of the grid goes dark. City by city, block by block, light by light.
      13. Nuclear reactors shut themselves down safely, but they still need power for cooling. Gas and coal plants run out of fuel deliveries.
      14. Only a few sources could last longer, like dams. At Hoover Dam in Nevada, falling water keeps spinning giant turbines, at least for a while.
      15. But with no grid to carry that power and nobody to control it, even those turbines would eventually trip and fall silent.
      16. So your first night alone, the city is black. Stars fill the sky like you've never seen them. Beautiful, and a little terrifying.
      17. Your freezer warms up. Your phone dies by tomorrow. The dream of free electricity lasts about as long as a long weekend.
      18. Myth two: you'd drive any Ferrari you want. Every showroom is open, every key is on the desk, and no speed limits exist anymore.
      19. You'd grab a red sports car, floor it down an empty highway, and feel like the king of the world.
      20. Reality check. Gasoline goes stale. In roughly six to twelve months, it starts to break down, leaving gummy deposits that clog engines.
      21. Worse, gas stations need electricity to pump fuel. No power means no pumps. You'd be stuck siphoning fuel by hand, one tank at a time.
      22. Batteries go dead after a few months of sitting. Tires slowly go flat. Rust creeps in. Even the fanciest car becomes a very expensive statue.
      23. And the roads? Crashed cars from the last day, fallen trees, and weeds cracking the pavement would block your path within a couple of years.
      24. So after a brief joyride, you end up on a bicycle. No fuel, no mechanic, no repair shop. Just your own legs.
      25. Honestly, a bike might be the best vehicle left. It never runs out of gas, and you can fix it yourself with a few tools.
      26. Myth three: supermarkets are endless food. Walk the aisles, grab whatever you want, and never pay a cent again.
      27. In the movies, the shelves stay perfect forever. Every apple is shiny, every steak is cold, and every bottle is full.
      28. Reality: refrigeration stops within a day or two. Meat and dairy spoil first, and soon the whole building smells like something you'd never forget.
      29. Fresh fruit and vegetables rot within weeks. Bread grows mold. Flies arrive, then rats and insects, and the store becomes their kingdom.
      30. But here's the good news. Canned food can last for years, often well past the printed date, as long as the can isn't damaged.
      31. Dry rice, pasta, and beans last even longer if you keep them dry. A smart survivor raids the canned aisle first, not the bakery.
      32. And then there's honey. Because it's so low in moisture and naturally acidic, honey basically never spoils. Jars from ancient Egyptian tombs were reportedly still edible.
      33. So you wouldn't starve right away. But the clock is ticking, because every can you open is one you can never replace.
      34. Eventually you'd have to farm, fish, or forage. And a person raised on delivery apps has never done that alone.
      35. But if you think food is hard, wait until you see what happens to the city itself. That's the next myth.
      36. Myth four: cities would look just like the movie, intact. Gleaming skyscrapers, clean streets, everything waiting for you to explore.
      37. Picture yourself alone in Times Square, spinning in the middle of the road with every billboard towering over you.
      38. Reality: a city is a machine that only works because thousands of people fix it every day. Stop, and nature takes over.
      39. Take New York. Its subway runs below the water table, and pumps remove about thirteen million gallons of water every single day.
      40. Those pumps need electricity. When the grid dies, the water starts to rise, quietly, in the dark, under your feet.
      41. Some estimates say that parts of the tunnels could flood in about thirty-six hours. A day and a half, and the subway becomes a river.
      42. Think about that. Thirty-six hours from silence to a flooded underground. And the ground above would start to weaken.
      43. Above the surface, rain does the rest. Gutters clog, roofs leak, and plants push up through every tiny crack in the pavement.
      44. Within a few years, trees grow through sidewalks. Within decades, bridges rust and many buildings begin to collapse. Within centuries, a forest.
      45. Writer Alan Weisman explored this in his book The World Without Us. His conclusion: cities decay much faster than anyone expects.
      46. So that perfect, intact skyline from the movies? It's a snapshot. It lasts for days, not for years.
      47. And yet, none of this is the biggest danger. The next myth is the one most people get completely backwards.
      48. Myth five: the real danger is wild animals. Lions, wolves, maybe a bear wandering through an empty shopping mall.
      49. Hollywood loves this one. Zoo animals escape, predators roam downtown, and you spend the whole movie hiding in a locked car.
      50. Reality: many pets and zoo animals wouldn't survive long. And most wild animals avoid humans anyway. They aren't hunting you.
      51. The real hazards are boring ones. An infected cut, food poisoning, a broken ankle with no doctor for a thousand miles.
      52. One small injury could be deadly. No ambulance, no hospital, no phone number to call. Every decision is entirely yours.
      53. But the biggest threat of all isn't a lion. It's the silence. It's loneliness.
      54. Humans are social animals. Our brains evolved to live in groups, and long-term isolation changes how our bodies actually work.
      55. Studies link chronic social isolation to health risks comparable to smoking fifteen cigarettes a day. Fifteen. Every day.
      56. That means higher risk of heart disease, depression, bad sleep, and early death. Loneliness isn't just sad. It's measurable.
      57. Remember Tom Hanks talking to a volleyball in Cast Away? That wasn't a joke. It's what a lonely brain does to survive.
      58. So you'd need routines. Wake at the same hour, keep a journal, exercise, read, build something every single day.
      59. Because surviving isn't just food and water. It's staying human when nobody is left to remind you what that means.
      60. Some nights you'd probably scream into an empty plaza. And the only answer would be your own echo.
      61. Now let's go back to the question, because I've been tricking you this whole video. Here's what no movie tells you.
      62. You pictured being the last human. But the question was the last person ON Earth. That's not the same thing.
      63. Right now, there are usually around ten people orbiting above you, aboard the International Space Station and China's Tiangong station.
      64. They float roughly two hundred fifty miles up, circling the planet every ninety minutes. They would watch every city go dark from above.
      65. And here's the best part. Their capsules are built to bring them home. Soyuz, Dragon, and Shenzhou can all carry crews back to Earth.
      66. Even with mission control silent, those capsules can in principle fly largely on their own, with parachutes doing the final braking.
      67. Meanwhile, down here, you sit under a black sky. Then one night, you notice a bright dot gliding across the stars.
      68. It doesn't blink like an airplane. It moves fast and steady. The space station is one of the brightest objects in the night sky.
      69. Imagine realizing that dot isn't a satellite. It's people. Someone looking back at you. Someone who might be coming home.
      70. So the truth is, the last person on Earth might not be alone for long. Hollywood forgot about everyone looking down.
      71. So tonight, when the sky gets dark, look up. Someone might be landing to find you.
      72. Tell me in the comments: which myth surprised you most? And would you have picked the Ferrari or the bicycle?
